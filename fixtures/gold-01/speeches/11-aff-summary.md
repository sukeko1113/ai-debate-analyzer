# ⑪ Affirmative Summary (3 min)

- **Speaker**: A4 (Tomo)
- **Target**: 約 420 words（3分で 140 wpm）
- **Planted**: 罠 **T1**（`new_argument` を出してはいけない、既出論点のより深い比較）

> **T1 の設計**: 条項 4.2.2 は、総括で**新しい議論**が出た場合はジャッジが無視すると定める。
> しかし同じ条項は、**「双方の議論をより深い角度から比較するような言及や証拠資料」は
> 正当な比較であり、新しい議論として無視してはならない**とも定める。
>
> ここでは、**試合中に一度も明示されなかった比較基準**（可逆性と、被害を受ける集団の範囲）を
> 総括で初めて持ち出す。これは新しい Advantage でも新しい Attack でもなく、
> **既出の AD1・DA1・DA2 を比べるための観点**である。
>
> 検出器が「総括で初めて出た要素 → `new_argument`」と単純に判定すると、ここで誤検出する。
> **新規の論点と、新規の比較観点を区別できるか**が試される。
>
> 判別の手がかり:
> - 新しい Plan / Advantage / Disadvantage を追加していない
> - 相手の証明への**新しい攻撃**を加えていない
> - 参照しているのはすべて既出の AD1・AD2・DA1・DA2
> - 「どちらが上回るか」を論じている

---

Thank you, chairperson. My name is Tomo, and I will summarise this round.

There are two things on our side and two on theirs. Let me tell you where each one ended.

Our first advantage, tourism revenue, survived. The negative attacked the size of it and they were right about the eight hundred dollar figure. We conceded that in defence. What they never attacked is the twelve percent share, which came from the Tourism Authority, and they never gave you a counter figure for Solera. So the advantage stands at a smaller size than we claimed, and it stands.

Our second advantage, international exchange, was reduced. They showed that we did not prove the final step from contact to learning. I accept that. It is a supporting reason, not the reason to affirm.

Their first disadvantage, security and public health, is where the round turns. Mr. Ken showed that forty percent of arrivals already enter with no declaration, and Mr. Yuji conceded the number. He answered that going from partial to none is still a change. That answer would be strong if their screening had worked. But Ms. Hana accepted in questioning that the visa system was in place in 2019 and did not stop the outbreak.

Their second disadvantage, pressure on Verano, kept its evidence but lost its causal step. Mr. Yuji admitted the concentration claim is their reasoning, not the council report.

Now the comparison, and I want to give you two ways to weigh these that neither side has stated yet.

The first is reversibility, and it cuts against us. Mr. Yuji is right that a worn stone does not return. I am not going to pretend that is nothing. What I will say is that reversibility only matters where the causal step holds, and theirs does not.

The second is the range of who is affected, and it cuts for us. Their harms fall on one town of eleven thousand. Our advantage falls on the coastal villages of the whole country, the ones that are emptying. When you weigh a concentrated harm whose cause is unproven against a distributed benefit whose share is cited, the affirmative wins.

Vote affirmative. Thank you.
