# ⑩ Negative Defense (3 min)

- **Speaker**: N3 (Yuji)
- **Target**: 約 420 words（3分で 140 wpm）
- **Planted**: なし（意図的にクリーン）

> **⑩ の役割上の制約**: 条項 2.1.6 により、否定ディフェンスは防御的なスピーチのみ。
> 新しい Disadvantage、肯定立論への新しいアタック、そして
> **直前の肯定ディフェンス⑨への再々反論も許されない**（それは否定総括⑫で行う）。
> 比較の観点を出すことは許される。
>
> ここでは ⑦ の攻撃に答えることに徹し、⑨ には触れない。
> **⑨ に触れれば違反になるが、ここでは仕込まない。**
> ⑫ に `new_argument`（#1）を仕込むので、違反を隣接させると
> どちらが検出されたのか切り分けにくくなる。

---

Thank you, chairperson. My name is Yuji, and I will defend our disadvantages.

Mr. Ken made four attacks. I take the security disadvantage first.

His main attack was uniqueness. He said forty percent already arrive without a declaration, so our harm is not created by the plan. In questioning he accepted that the declaration from the remaining sixty percent has value. Partial value, he said. So the argument reduces to this: we currently receive health information about sixty percent of arrivals, and under their plan we receive it about none of them. That is not the same situation. That is a change from partial coverage to no coverage, and Mr. Ken conceded the direction of it himself.

His second attack was that a refusal is not a prevented crime. That is true as a matter of logic, and I will not overstate our evidence. But notice what he said when I asked him directly. I asked whether a person with a serious criminal record is more likely to offend than a person without one. He said he would not accept it without a figure. Members of the audience may draw their own conclusion about a position that requires a study before accepting that.

On the community disadvantage, he said our concentration claim is our reasoning rather than the report. Correct, and I said so in questioning. But his alternative is also reasoning, and it is weaker. He told you the coast has capacity. I asked for a figure. He had none. Between two pieces of reasoning, ours starts from a council report showing four hundred thousand visitors against eleven thousand residents. His starts from nothing measured.

His last attack was that the stairway wore during a decade when the visa existed. That is right, and it is why our disadvantage is about the rate of wear rather than the fact of it. Eleven millimetres came from four hundred thousand visitors a year. Their own case says arrivals grow by a third.

Now the comparison.

The affirmative measures their case in money and ours in caution. But our disadvantage has the harder of the two properties: it is irreversible. A worn stone is not restored and a resident who leaves does not return. Their economic effect, if it fails to appear, costs us a year of growth we did not have anyway. If ours appears, it costs us something we cannot buy back.

That asymmetry is the reason to negate. Thank you.
