Thank you, chairperson. My name is Mina, and I will present the affirmative constructive speech.

Our plan is as follows. The Republic of Meridia will abolish tourist visa requirements for the nationals of all countries, starting from the next fiscal year. Tourist stays of up to ninety days will be permitted without prior application. No other immigration rule is changed by this plan.

Today I will present three advantages. Tourism revenue, international exchange, and regional employment.

My first advantage is tourism revenue.

Let me begin with the present situation. According to the Meridia Tourism Authority Statistics, sixty percent of the countries in the world still need a tourist visa to enter Meridia. Applicants must wait about three weeks, and they must pay forty-five dollars. Travel agencies in our region report that many customers simply choose another destination rather than wait. Our share of regional tourism is four percentage points below the regional average, and that gap has not closed in ten years. Last month one of our largest travel partners in the east closed its Meridia desk.

Next, the effect of our plan. When the barrier disappears, visitors increase. According to the Solera Government Immigration White Paper of 2023, our neighbour Solera abolished tourist visas in 2021, and arrivals grew by thirty-four percent within two years. Meridia and Solera share the same climate, the same flight network, and a similar hotel capacity. There is no reason to expect a smaller effect here.

Now the importance. Tourism is twelve percent of our national economy. Last year we received one point two million visitors, and each visitor spends about eight hundred dollars, so this industry is worth nine hundred and sixty million dollars to us. Professor Chen, a tourism economist at the University of Meridia, states that a ten percent increase in arrivals would create four thousand new jobs in coastal villages such as Kestrel Bay. These are not jobs in the capital. They are jobs in the places that need them most.

My second advantage is international exchange.

The present situation is that young people in Meridia have very few chances to meet people from other countries. Our schools want to host short exchange programmes, but the three week application period makes a one week visit impossible to arrange. Teachers have told the ministry that they gave up.

The effect of our plan is straightforward. More visitors mean more face to face contact. Visitors who enjoy their stay come back, and some of them bring their schools and their towns with them. Sister city partnerships begin with individual visits.

The importance is long term. Students who meet foreign visitors while they are still at school understand the world differently. This is an educational benefit that lasts for a generation.

My third advantage is regional employment.

The present situation is that our coastal villages are losing population every year. Young people leave because there is no work. The fishing industry alone cannot hold them.

The effect of our plan is that visitors spread beyond the capital once travel becomes easy. When a traveller does not need to plan three weeks ahead, that traveller can decide to take the coastal train on the morning of the third day.

The importance is that jobs stay in the regions where people already live, instead of moving to the capital. A village that keeps its young families keeps its school, and a village that keeps its school does not disappear.

For these three reasons, tourism revenue, international exchange, and regional employment, the resolution should be affirmed. Thank you.
